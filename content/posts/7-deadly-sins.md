I always thought my mind was a weapon. Every note I hit on the piano, every line of code I wrote, every sketch that came alive on my canvas, every argument I dissected in philosophy or psychology — it all made me feel untouchable. *People called me talented. I called myself unstoppable.*  

Pride was the first taste, the sweet poison that said: *“You are above the rest.”*  

They made me president of the college tech club after I built a landing page that looked like art and a token-gated riddle that guarded admissions. Validation felt like oxygen. I started answering first, dressing sharper, making sure my laughter echoed like I already lived in the future. The crown was invisible, but I wore it heavy.  

Then greed braided itself into pride. *“More,”* it whispered. Sponsorships, freelance gigs, grants, gambling streaks I swore were just algorithms dressed as luck. I hoarded opportunities, took contracts I couldn’t finish, siphoned favors, outsourced promises. Money wasn’t money—it was proof. Proof I was still ascending, still untouchable.  

Lust followed fast. Not just bodies, but the hunger to be *wanted.* At underground gigs and hackathons, I drowned in late-night collabs that always blurred. The applause, the whispers, the praise—they all melted into the same drug. I wanted to be consumed. I let myself be consumed. And yet every morning I woke emptier than before.  

Envy slid in quieter than the rest. A kid younger, sharper, moving faster. I congratulated him out loud, but in the dark I was scraping his release notes, plotting flaws, rehearsing how to bury him with brilliance. *“You must outshine or you will vanish.”* That voice didn’t let me sleep. I didn’t realize it was already corroding me.  

Gluttony made my life look like a warzone. Pizza boxes stacked, whiskey bottles rolling, code windows layered over unfinished canvases. I binged practice until my wrists hurt, binged theories until my brain hummed, binged attention until the silence felt like death. Creation became consumption; I was both artist and addict.  

Wrath came like fire in my throat. I burned collaborators for typos, scorched friends for doubts, slammed doors on mentors who dared to question me. *“Passion,”* I called it. But passion doesn’t leave craters. Wrath does. The night Jatin—my labmate, quiet, careful—asked me to slow down, I laughed in his face. That laugh still tastes like ash.  

Sloth didn’t arrive—it settled. Weeks without code. Months without music. Ideas rotting in notebooks. Emails I couldn’t open. I sat in a room thick with dust and ash, staring at blank walls, paralyzed. Pride, greed, lust, envy, gluttony, wrath—they had all eaten me alive, and sloth buried the bones.  

And yet—there was a twist. One morning, an envelope slid under my door: *an invitation to a global creativity symposium.* They wanted me—not the ruins, not the ashes, but the mind that could braid code, music, psychology, and art into one. They didn’t know about the hunger. They only saw potential.  

I stared at it, pulse hammering, palms slick. Pride whispered: *“Rise again.”*  
Greed hissed: *“Take it all.”*  
Lust purred: *“They will want you.”*  
Envy taunted: *“Others will already be better.”*  
Gluttony urged: *“Devour it all.”*  
Wrath growled: *“Dominate them.”*  
Sloth sighed: *“Stay here. Let it rot.”*  

I took a breath. For the first time, I felt all seven sins speaking at once—an orchestra of hunger inside me. And yet, I stood. Sneakers scuffed cracked pavement. Fingers itched to create.  

The world isn’t ready for me.  
I’m not ready for the world.  
But I will meet it anyway.
