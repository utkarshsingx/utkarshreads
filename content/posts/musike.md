There are people who listen to music,  
and then there are people who *become* music.  
For someone who belong to the latter, it feels less like choice and more like possession.  

Pressing that play button, I’m not in my room anymore. It is I, in someone’s memory, walking barefoot through a rainy street in Tokyo as a violin sings about loss. I am sitting in a smoky bar in Berlin as a saxophone bends time into something fragile, lying under a mango tree in Gorakhnath as a sitar turns silence into prayer. Music doesn’t just travel to me—I travel *through* it.  

Nietzsche once said: *“Without music, life would be a mistake.”* let me: without music, I would not have my piece complete.  

Every style has its own doorway.  
**Classical**, a cathedral where every note is a stone lifted by invisible hands. **Jazz**, drunk conversation that somehow makes more sense than sobriety ever could. **Rock** feels like a rebellion that still believes salvation is possible. **Electronic** being circuitry learning how to dream, and last my **Lo-fi** hits like memory, fragile and glitching, refusing to fade.  

Instruments they, they aren’t objects, storytellers? Yes. A piano doesn’t just play, nope, it opens veins of nostalgia. A violin doesn’t just sing, it cries for every century it has survived. Drums don’t just beat, they remind me of the heart I’ve been ignoring. You know, even silence, wrapped between lyrics, has the weight of entire galaxies.  

Lyrics, when they’re honest, are more than words. They’re *x-rays of the soul.* and they do pass right through me, leaving no wound but a permanent mark. I live for those moments when a single line makes me feel like I’ve been living in someone else’s chest, watching the world through their ribs.  

For me, this isn’t entertainment, it’s reincarnation. Each song is another life I get to live without dying.  

So when you see me with earphones on, don’t think I’m escaping.  
I’m arriving.  
To places I’ll never find on maps,  
to faces I’ll never meet but will always know,  
to souls I’ll borrow for three minutes and thirty-eight seconds  
before I return, changed.  

Because music isn’t something I listen to.  
Music is something I *become.*
